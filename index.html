1966年，中国进入文化大革命。 In 1966, China entered the Wénhuà Dàgémìng (“Cultural Revolution”).

学校停课，书籍被烧，知识分子被批斗、下放、迁散。对许多亲历那个年代的人来说，这是一段不愿回忆、不再提起的创伤。 Schools closed. Books were burned. Intellectuals were denounced, sent down, and scattered. For many who lived through that time, it is a trauma they are unwilling to remember or speak of again.

但不说，不代表没有发生过。 But silence does not mean it never happened.

1944年，沈蕙兰生于安徽芜湖的知识分子家庭，后被下放农村再教育。 Born in 1944 into an intellectual family in Wuhu, Anhui, Shen Huilan was later sent to a farm for re-education.

这是她的故事，也是那一代人中无数个没有被记录下来的故事之一。 This is her story, and one of the countless stories from her generation that were never recorded.

远处有人叫她的名字。 Someone called her name in the distance.

沈蕙兰突然想起明天的劳动任务。她想起父亲、母亲和惠国。 Shen Huilan suddenly remembered tomorrow’s work. She thought of Father, Mother, and Huiguo.

她最后看了一眼那条街。然后轻轻地，把门关上了。 She looked at the street one last time.Then she gently closed the door.

七十年代末，事情开始松动，她重返皖南中学，回到讲台。 In the late 1970s, as restrictions eased, she returned to Wannan Middle School and began teaching again.

又过了几年，她做了语文教研组长，每年讲《孔雀东南飞》，讲屈原，讲她自己喜欢的一切。 A few years later, she led the Chinese teaching group. Each year she taught The Peacock Flies Southeast, Qu Yuan, and everything she loved.

三十一年，春去秋来，直到退休。 Thirty-one years passed, season after season, until she retired.

很久之后，她才知道，门的另一边是一个叫格林威治村的地方。她一直记得。 Much later, she learned the place beyond the door was Greenwich Village.She never forgot.

退休那年，学生送给她一株君子兰。她把它放在窗台上，养得很好，年年开花。 When she retired, her students gave her a clivia.She kept it on the windowsill. It bloomed every year.
